Working Backwards that doesn't stop at the press release.

Amazon's PR/FAQ decides whether to build. It ends there by design — and that handoff is where the method dies: the narrative gets admired, then someone opens a blank ticket and starts from zero.

This runs all nine stages — press release, FAQs, demo, docs, telemetry, requirements, release plan, readiness — with a critic gating each one. Every requirement cites the paragraph it came from, because a requirement that can't cite anything is a fairly precise definition of scope creep.

§1 — How it runs

Three roles, one loop, per stage.

An author produces the artifact. A critic reviews it against a fixed rubric — and is architecturally barred from ruling on what it can't know. A human gates every transition. Session state lives on disk, so a run resumes days later.

The critic judges presence and provenance, never truth. It can tell whether a claim is made, specific, and sourced — not whether it's correct. Confident verdicts on unknowable things simulate a review meeting without the knowledge that made review meetings valuable; that failure mode is designed out, not prompted away.

§3 — What the FAQ stage found

Six blockers, before a single requirement existed.

This is the pipeline's payback moment. At Stage 2 — before requirements, before design — the hostile-question banks surfaced six things that reshape the plan. Each is a question with an owner, never a finding: the skill cannot assess legal exposure, so it names the person who can, and it has no way to mark its own question resolved.

BLK-05 and BLK-06 came from the two internal-FAQ questions that reliably pay: who owns the system this depends on, and can we enumerate the affected population retroactively. Both made half the release plan unschedulable — which is the honest output, not a failure. Full records: BLOCKERS.md

§4 — What it doesn't know

A question-generation engine, honest about it.

Amazon's PR/FAQ works because it's a meeting — the people in the room are the context. A solo user plus a model replaces the document, not the room. So every claim carries provenance, and the session ends with a completeness meter, not a confidence trick. Toggle the runs:
