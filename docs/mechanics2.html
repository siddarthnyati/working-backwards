The 90-second version
  1. "You say what’s on your mind and it’s recorded verbatim. Mine was a feature request wearing a problem costume — four challenges later it was an observed cohort with a kill condition."
  2. "The model writes the press release — the ending first. Where I had no number, it wrote [NEEDS EVIDENCE] instead of inventing one. Every claim carries its provenance tag."
  3. "A separate judge call reviews every stage. It failed my first draft — ‘everyone is not a customer’ — and on the two questions it can’t know, strategy and feasibility, it’s only allowed to ask, never to rule."
  4. "The FAQ stage asks the hostile questions on purpose. Finance’s — ‘are you asking me to approve a number you can’t state?’ — became a blocker with an owner instead of an ambush in review."
  5. "If the press release claims it, telemetry must measure it — or the claim comes out. The claim map is what keeps the launch honest."
  6. "Requirements only exist if they cite a paragraph, and a script — not the model — verifies every citation resolves. The one resting on an assumption is flagged, never hidden."
  7. "It ends with a scoped GO: two slices ship today, one needs exactly one meeting. I decide, it records. I don’t trust the model — I trust the chain of custody around it."
