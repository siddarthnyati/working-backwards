The requirements — all seven, engineer-ready.

Card anatomy, top to bottom: the user story (whose life changes), the source (which paragraph demands this — machine-verified by verify_sources.py), a testable statement, Given / When / Then observable from outside the system, and the honest footer — out of scope (what readers would assume is included and isn't) and dependencies by id. Flags travel with the card: an assumption from intake is still an assumption here, four stages later.

Definition of Ready, enforced by pipeline. Every card above passed the same gate before reaching this page: its Source: resolves (checked by script, not by promise) · the statement is one testable sentence · the Then is observable from outside the system · it lands in exactly one release slice · anything resting on an assumption says so on its face. The team never receives a vague ask, and scope creep has a working definition: a requirement that can't cite anything.

What the team is deliberately not asked to build yet.

Two slices start today with nobody's permission. Two stay NOT SCHEDULABLE — and each names the non-engineering owner and the exact meeting that unblocks it. No dates on blocked work: a date on a blocked slice commits engineers to a shape nobody agreed to.

The engineering-manager reading of this table: your team never inherits an ambiguous or politically blocked ticket. The open questions stay attached to the finance, compliance, and platform owners who can actually answer them — and the sprint starts on R1 and R2, which are useful even if the email program never ships.

The whole point.

  • ✓Every requirement cites the paragraph that demands it — verified by script, on every run.
  • ✓Every Then is observable from outside the system — testable without reading anyone's mind.
  • ✓Every assumption is flagged on the card that inherits it — nothing rests on a guess silently.
  • ✓Every blocked item names its owner and its unblocking meeting — no dates on other people's decisions.

That's the standardization: the process ends where engineering begins, with nothing lost in translation on the way.
