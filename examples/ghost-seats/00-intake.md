# Intake — Ghost Seats

Session: `ghost-seats` · Mode: `full` · Date: 2026-08-05

> **Fictional worked example.** No connection to any real company, product, or dataset.
> Generated by running the `working-backwards` skill in Full mode, deliberately with no
> `wb/context/` pack, so the example shows the honest degraded case.

## Trigger

Analysis of platform deprovisioning events found that the removal event fires locally but
does not always propagate to connected third-party integrations. `[OBSERVED: deprovisioning
event log, four-quarter export]`

The failure is silent in both directions. No error is returned to the platform, and no error
is surfaced to the admin. The workspace audit log records the local removal, so the admin's
only available evidence says the user was removed. `[OBSERVED: audit log writes on the local
removal event only; there is no propagation-confirmation field]`

Roughly 4,200 accounts per quarter show the pattern. `[OBSERVED: same export]`

## Customer

**Segment:** IT and workspace administrators at mid-market and enterprise B2B SaaS customers
— 200 to 5,000 seats — who have three or more third-party integrations connected and who are
subject to a periodic access review, whether contractual, audit-driven, or internal policy.

**Size / shape:** You could build a list of ten of them from the customers who have both
(a) more than two active integration connections and (b) a signed DPA with a revocation
timeliness clause. Both are queryable today. `[OBSERVED: both fields exist in the account
record]`

**Why this population and not all admins:** an admin with one integration and no access
review either doesn't have the failure or has no mechanism that would ever reveal it. The
harm lands on admins who are accountable for proving revocation happened.

**Not this product's customer:** end users (they never see this), single-integration
workspaces, and free-tier workspaces with no connected integrations.

Worth noting because it changes the recommendation: nothing forces a decision date, so

## Who must agree

Roles, not names.

- Platform engineering lead — owns the propagation handler
- Partnerships lead — owns the integration contracts
- Privacy counsel / DPO — owns the erasure-obligation determination
- Security lead — owns the exposure assessment
- Legal counsel — owns the contractual exposure and disclosure question
- VP Product — owns whether this is funded this quarter

Six roles and no single owner is itself the finding that made this a Full-mode session.
