The trial that ends politely

Nobody gets charged by surprise: you'll know before we bill you, and cancelling takes one tap.

Why now

38%of refunds cite "forgot to cancel / didn't know"[OBSERVED: refund log]
#1support category in week 3 of every cohort: "surprise charge"[OBSERVED: ticket tags]
71%of eventual refunders never opened the app after trial day 3[OBSERVED: activity × billing]
?charged users who are surprised but silent — never measured[UNKNOWN → R1]

Finance believes reminders will cost conversions; product believes they won't. That claim is [ASSUMED — C5] on both sides — so this ships with the holdback experiment both sides agreed to be bound by (REQ-D2).

Who it affects

The one who forgot

trial users who stopped opening the app by day 3 and get charged on day 14 — the forgot-cohort

Today: discovers the charge on a bank statement, feels tricked, writes the one-star review, then asks for the refund. We return the money. The review stays.

After

told twice before the charge; out in one tap

A T-48h email and an in-app banner, each with two equal buttons: keep it, or cancel in one tap. Receipts always show the next renewal date and price.

The experience

Email · T-48h
"Your trial ends Thursday — keep it or cancel in one tap." Price, date, two equal buttons. No guilt copy.
In-app banner · day 12+
The backstop for unopened email: date, price, same two buttons. Returns daily until decided.
One-tap cancel
One screen, immediate confirmation, data kept 30 days. Never more steps than subscribing took — asserted in CI.
Failure state
If the reminder send fails, the miss is logged and visible — the charge is never silently excused, and the failure never silently hidden.

What it deliberately does not do

No win-back discounts in the cancel flow — nothing upstream asks for it, and the judge held it at the door. No proactive refunds for past charges until legal answers (BLK-03). No reminders beyond the first charge — scoped to the moment of harm.
